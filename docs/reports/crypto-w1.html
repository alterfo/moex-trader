Crypto transfer of the 46 registered lines

The MOEX programme holds 46 registered research lines. Each was mapped to a tradable crypto analogue and scored on USDⓈ-M perpetuals with that market's own costs: exchange fees, spread charged by liquidity tier, and funding charged over the holding period. Result of the first stage: no candidate, two leads. The order-flow book earns 13.7 bps a day net with t 2.89, and the one-month momentum book 21.6 bps a day with t 2.18 — both below the registered gate of absolute t 3.3 gate, and two fifths of the flow book's net is funding carry rather than signal.

01What was asked, and the order of operations

The request was to check all 46 strategies on crypto markets, with the costs of those markets accounted for. "All 46" is read as the 46 plan files the programme registers, each mapped to a tradable analogue or marked not transferable with the reason. The mapping lives in the plan; this note reports what ran.

The order matters. Each line is priced first, not backtested: a book that turns over a fraction t of notional a side per day must earn 2·t·c before it earns anything, where c is the all-in cost per side. A row whose gross edge sits below that line is closed without a backtest, because a backtest would only decorate the arithmetic. Ten cells had a plausible cost path; they were run once, on a frozen panel.

Three things decide a crypto verdict before any signal does: the fee tier, the spread by liquidity tier, and funding — which is not a fee but a payment on the held position, and at these horizons it is larger than the entire fee budget.

02The panel, and what it cannot show

Prices come from the venue's own monthly bulk dumps: daily klines and the funding-rate history of every USDⓈ-M perpetual that ever traded, delisted contracts included, so the cross-section is not a survivor panel. Tokenised equities, commodities and index contracts are excluded — 239 of 942 live contracts are not crypto, and a tokenised NVDA would smuggle an equity factor into a crypto cross-section.

What the data cannot show, and this bounds everything below: there are no order books. The flow signal here is the kline field taker_buy_quote_volume / quote_volume — an aggressive-flow share over 20 days — not the book-imbalance signal the MOEX A2 test used. The spread and impact in the cost model are liquidity-tier assumptions, not measurements. Both gaps are closable from the same public source and are registered as the next pull.

03What crypto actually costs

Perpetual futures are cheap to trade and expensive to hold. The taker fee at the retail tier is 0.05 per cent a side, half the MOEX model's 0.05 commission plus 0.05 spread plus 0.05 slippage; the spread widens with illiquidity, and funding accrues every hour or every eight hours for as long as the position stays on.

A 40-day position at the median rate pays about 121 bps in funding; the same position pays about 13 bps in fees.

That is why the programme's 10-40 day horizons cannot be transferred as directional books, and why every cell below is dollar-neutral by construction.

05The one thing above the bar, and why it is still only a lead

Order flow — the share of aggressive buying in a contract's turnover, averaged over 20 days — is the only cell that clears the Bonferroni bar for ten trials. It is also the one that most obviously tempts a false conclusion, so it was put through ten single-change variants, a placebo, and a carry decomposition.

Break-even: 83.7 bps a side. Charged on average: 12.3 bps a side.

The economics are not the obstacle for once: a book with this edge per unit of turnover can pay crypto costs several times over. The obstacles are statistical and structural — t 2.89 against a 3.3 gate, a single venue, one window, and a net that is two fifths carry. By the programme's own convention that is a lead, not a candidate, and it stays a lead until a forward record and a measured-spread re-read exist.

07What would change the verdict

  1. Measure the spread instead of assuming it. Best-bid and offer history exists per contract per day; sampling it by pair and hour replaces the tier assumption in every cost line, and doubles as the execution-timing measurement the programme wants anyway.
  2. Rebuild the flow signal from the book. One-minute depth snapshots give an imbalance series closer to the MOEX A2 test than the aggressive-flow proxy used here, and settle whether the lead is flow or carry.
  3. Split the carry out. A funding-neutral construction, or one restricted to contracts with near-zero funding, separates the two halves of the net without touching the signal.
  4. Walk it forward. Eight weeks of paper records on one venue at frozen parameters, which is the only path the programme accepts from lead to candidate.

Until those four exist, the honest summary of transferring 46 MOEX research lines to crypto is this page: four of them have a measurable answer, one of those answers is that the market trends and does not reverse, two are leads worth following, and the other 41 were never about a signal that costs could not eat.
